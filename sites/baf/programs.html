The working model

Continuous returns, not one good week in November

The expo is the front door. Behind it sit four programmes that keep earning: facilitation, the directory, the outreach programme, and Farm Aid.

Business facilitation Directory Outreach Farm Aid Subjects

Programme 01

Business facilitation

One question drives it: who needs what, and who can supply it? Everything else is a record that keeps that answer current.

Suppliers log

The exhibitor record

Every exhibitor and service provider is entered once: business name, contact, district, category, what they supply, capacity, price band and whether they can supply all year. This is the supply side of every match.

  • ✓ Built at registration, not after the expo
  • ✓ Doubles as the paid directory listing
  • ✓ Scored for credit history once orders clear
Demand log

Every visitor's answer

A contextualised questionnaire turns a visitor into a lead: what they farm, what they came to buy, quantity, budget, district, timing, and whether they need credit. Three capture routes, one record:

  • 1 QR form — visitor scans, fills on their own phone
  • 2 Printed objective form — information desk, tick boxes, no literacy barrier
  • 3 Exhibitor marshal — logs the lead at the stand on the live site
Credit desk

Creditors & guarantors

Credit is attached to two records, never to a promise: the demand log entry it funds, and the suppliers log entry that fulfils it. A named creditor or guarantor stands behind each one, with the amount, the terms and the relationship recorded.

The result is a book that a SACCO, a cooperative or a lender can actually read.

Backend

Admin, roles & analytics

One backend with role-based access — registrar, marshal, credit officer, analyst — and dashboards on leads per exhibitor, conversion to order, credit exposure by guarantor, and category gaps in supply.

Reports are what make the next expo cheaper to sell: you can show an exhibitor exactly what the last one returned.

Status: the public capture forms are live on the expo site; the admin backend and dashboard layer are specified in the network blueprint and stand up before the first paid listing cycle.

Programme 02

The print & online business directory

A yellow-pages for Ugandan agribusiness: the printed edition that travels to parish meetings and the expo gate, and the online edition that can be searched, updated and sold into all year.

  • ✓ Categorised listings — inputs, seed, feed, vet, fabrication, transport, processing, finance, training
  • ✓ Verified contact, district and opening hours
  • ✓ Paid placement and featured categories fund the print run
  • ✓ Every listing is also a suppliers-log entry
Browse the current listings

Why it keeps paying

The directory is the only asset here that a buyer, a supplier and a financier all need at once. Reprints, category sponsorship and featured slots repeat every year without rebuilding anything.

And it is the natural landing page for every QR code printed on a bag, a receipt or a form.

Programme 03

The outreach programme

The expo ends; the log does not. Outreach is how the network reaches past the gate — parish by parish, layer by layer, using the clan structure that already convenes people.

ConveneMeet at the level that already gathers: oluggya, olunyiriri, omutuba, essiga.
EnrolRegister suppliers, farmers and buyers on the spot, on paper or phone.
TrainShort, practical sessions — records, feeds, storage, credit discipline.
ReturnMatched supply, matched demand, and a directory that grows with every visit.

Busolya Biz Network is the name this programme trades under; Bataka Trade Network is where the volume goes.

Programme 04

Farm Aid — the exit option

Dormant arable land is the largest idle asset in the network. Farm Aid puts it to work without selling it: the project finances and sets up the farm, trains a manager, and shares proceeds until it has recouped at least 17× its outlay — then the manager takes the farm on.

1. Land & agreement

A dormant plot is identified and a written agreement covers use, term, shares, obligations and the exit. Nothing is planted before the paper is signed.

2. Set-up & manager

Farm Aid funds inputs, tools and start-up. The farmer — or whoever they delegate — is installed as manager and trained in husbandry, farm management and farm accountancy.

3. Shares & exit

Proceeds are shared on recorded books. Once Farm Aid has recouped a minimum of 17× its returns, the manager exits into ownership of a running, documented farm.

Commercial terms are confirmed per farm in the written agreement. The 17× figure is the floor for recoupment, not a promise of yield.

Teaching programme

Education & skilling at the expo

Visitors are taught, not just sold to. Farm Aid is the anchor subject — how to prepare a manager, how to run a farm, how to keep a farm's accounts. Eight more run alongside it, and more are welcome.

Subject 1

Farm Aid: managing a farm

Selecting and preparing a manager; husbandry, planning, labour and supervision.

Subject 2

Farm accountancy

Simple books a manager can keep daily: inputs, labour, sales, stock, and the farm's true cost.

Subject 3

Abasolya, Bataka & clan layers

Ekika to enju, the councils, the Katikkiro at each layer, and what allegiance to the Kingdom carries.

Subject 4

Land, tenure & dues

Mailo, kibanja and busuulu explained plainly, and what to put in a farm or tenancy agreement.

Subject 5

Credit, savings & guarantors

How a SACCO reads a member, what a guarantor really signs, and how to build a borrowing record.

Subject 6

Agronomy clinic

Cassava, banana, coffee and maize: planting material, spacing, pests and the season's decisions.

Subject 7

Livestock & poultry

Feeds, housing, disease control, breeding and the records that show a herd is growing.

Subject 8

Post-harvest & standards

Drying, storage, grading, packaging and what a buyer or a certifier will check before paying.

Subject 9

Digital & market access

QR forms, the online directory, WhatsApp selling, and how to be found by a buyer outside your district.

Open call: a tenth subject can be added by any exhibitor or institution willing to teach it. Proposals go through the contact page before the programme goes to print.
