The root of the name

Abasolya, Abataka and the layers of a clan

Busolya is a name with an owner. This page is the plain-language account the expo teaches from: who the Abasolya are, who counts as an Omutaka, and how a clan is stacked from ekika down to enju.

Abasolya

Ab'Obusolya — the clan chiefs

The Abasolya are the clan chiefs of Buganda. Obusolya is the apex — the roof-tip of the house. The name places the office at the top of the clan's structure, not beside it: the people who carry the clan's authority, its land, its custom and its duty to the Kingdom.

Buganda counts more than fifty clans. Each clan is a whole institution with a head, a council, a totem, a drum, a duty at the royal court, and land to go with it.

Bataka

Omutaka — the land holder

An Omutaka (plural Bataka) is a land owner who pays allegiance to the Kingdom of Buganda. That allegiance is not only ceremonial — it carries servitude where custom requires it, affiliation to a clan and a lineage, and the land dues owed to the Kingdom.

In practice the word is used for the clan heads who hold land under the Kingdom. The relation is reciprocal: land and standing come with obligation, and both are recorded.

The stack

Six layers, one clan

Each layer sits inside the one above it. Read from the top down: the clan, its hearth-stone, the tree, the line, the compound, the homestead.

LayerLiterallyWhat it is
Ekika / Kika the clan The whole clan — totem, head, council, court duty and land. The widest circle a Musolya belongs to.
Essiga / Ssiga hearth-stone One of the three stones that hold the cooking pot. The sub-clan: the hearth at which the clan is fed and divided.
Omutuba / Mutuba the fig/bark-cloth tree The tree the bark cloth is cut from. The branch of descent under an essiga — named, planted and known.
Olunyiriri / Lunyiriri a line, a queue The lineage: the line of descent that marks who follows whom.
Oluggya / Luggya the front yard The large front compound where food is dried and meetings are held. In Buganda the front yard is public and generous; the back is for the family. Office holders at this level plan for gatherings.
Enju / Nju the homestead The great-grandfather's line — commonly counted to about the fifth generation, higher or lower where descent is stronger. The innermost circle.

Governance

Every layer has a Katikkiro

Each layer is headed by a Katikkiro — a premier who represents the head of that layer and can be removed by that head. The remaining offices at the layer are filled by agreement: candidates are proposed, the head and the council settle it.

That structure is why a farmers' network can be built on it. A layer already has a convener, a council, a meeting ground and a register of who belongs. Business facilitation plugs into an institution that is already running.

  • ✓ Head of the layer — holds authority, appoints and removes the premier
  • ✓ Katikkiro — represents the head, runs the layer's business
  • ✓ Council — offices settled by proposal and consensus, not inheritance alone
